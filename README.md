# Simulate: a what-if tab for ORO's process designer

**Live demo:** https://mohith26.github.io/oro-labs-process-simulator/

Unofficial concept demo by Mohith Gajjela. Not affiliated with ORO Labs. Synthetic data.

## What it is

ORO lets a procurement team change a process in minutes: reorder steps, run checks in parallel, let an agent replace a reviewer. ORO Studio can now design a whole flow from a conversation. What nobody sees before publishing is **what the change will do to cycle time and to the people who work the queues**, especially when volume is about to jump (the SAP Ariba extension goes GA in November 2026).

Simulate is a third tab next to *Tasks* and *Sub Processes* in the ORO Administration process designer. It learns the process from its own request log, replays real requests through the draft, and shows:

- median and 90th-percentile intake-to-done time, published vs draft, with a 90% range,
- queue load for each reviewer group (work arriving ÷ hours scheduled),
- per-step time and waiting on the canvas,
- which parallel branch is the critical one,
- the risk side of skipping a human step (how often reviewers corrected the agent),
- a **backtest**: the model was fitted only on older requests and checked against the most recent three weeks.

Demo process: *Proposal Risk-Domain Triage* (the process ORO built live in its ORO Studio demo): triage agent → compliance reviewer confirms domains → sanctions screening → bank verification callback → email/domain check → compliance exception decision.

## How it works

- `src/sim.js` — discrete-event simulation (binary-heap event queue). Human steps go to FIFO queues worked by named people, each with their own daily working window and absence calendar; work rolls across days and weekends. External steps (screening provider, supplier confirming bank details) run on calendar or business time. Parallel steps join on the last branch.
- `src/whatif.js` — learns everything from the log, never from the generator's parameters:
  - each person's working window (1st/99th percentile of when they pick up and finish tasks),
  - presence: of the weekdays with a backlog waiting at their window open, the share on which they picked up work,
  - hands-on work time per task, counted only inside that person's window on days they were active,
  - empirical distributions for agent, screening and supplier delays.

  `forecast()` replays the window's real arrivals (plus 2 weeks of warm-up), bootstraps every empirical distribution per run for parameter uncertainty, and reports the median and 5–95% range over 40 runs. Extra volume is added by resampling real requests within the same days.
- `src/generate.js` — the synthetic "truth": arrivals with Monday peaks and a quarter-end surge, lognormal step times, people with fixed windows and random days out. The same engine generates the log, so the backtest checks whether *fitting* recovers behaviour, not the engine itself.
- `index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js` — ORO Administration's process designer rebuilt from ORO's own product recording, with the Simulate tab, annotated canvas and results column.

## Tests

```
node --test test/whatif.test.mjs
node scripts/report.mjs
```

8 tests: business calendar (weekend rollover, office minutes, window minutes); engine FIFO and parallel join on hand-built cases; generator determinism and completion; the fit recovers every person's window exactly, presence within 0.06 of the realized absence calendar, and work-time medians within 10%; the backtest covers the actual p50 and p90 with KS < 0.12; what-ifs move the right way (volume pushes p90 out, treasury time pulls it back, parallel callback is critical, naive estimate is blind to volume); simulated utilization matches offered load within 12%; seeded determinism.

## Honest limitations

- Synthetic data, and the log is generated by the same kind of engine the forecast uses, so model mismatch is milder than in a real tenant. Real logs have priorities, reassignment, delegation, holidays, and people who work several queues at once.
- Hands-on work time is inferred from pickup and completion timestamps. If people leave tasks open while doing other work, the inferred work time is too long and forecasts are pessimistic. Real ORO tasks may have better signals (opened, saved, reassigned).
- Presence is inferred from activity, so a person who is in but ignores the queue looks absent. That is usually what matters for cycle time, but it is not a staffing fact.
- Extra volume is resampled from the same weeks, so it keeps the current mix of categories and flagged rates.
- Skipping a step changes risk, not just time. The demo shows the historical correction rate; a real rollout should certify it statistically and monitor it.
